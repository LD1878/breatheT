The building

Stone that was built to dry

Solid Cotswold stone walls, cob partitions, and the original lime plaster. A house like this was never sealed. Moisture moves into the wall in winter and leaves again when the weather turns.

A closed insulation board would have stopped that. On a Grade I building that is not a risk worth taking, and it is not one Historic England will accept.

What we did

Lined from the inside. The street face stayed.

A breathable mineral lining, thin enough to keep the rooms in proportion, open enough for the stone to dry.

  1. 01

    Read the wall first

    Stone, cob and lime, not a cavity. Thickness was set by what the wall could still dry through, not by a target U-value on its own.

  2. 02

    A vapour-open lining

    PerliTherm, expanded perlite bound with hydraulic lime, is the insulating body. The build-up stays mineral all the way to the finish.

  3. 03

    Historic England’s bar

    The work was done from the inside, so the listed elevation did not change. The result had to satisfy the strictest consent requirements, not just a heat-loss calculation.

The outcome

Warm enough to feel. Open enough to last.

U-value 0.22 after the lining. Heating bills down 52%. The stone, the cob and the lime plaster are still doing the job they were built for.

That is the point of the system on a building this sensitive: a measurable change in the rooms, and no change you can see from the lane.
